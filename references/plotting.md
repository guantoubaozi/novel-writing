# Plotting

## Establish the story engine

1. Condense the premise into protagonist, disruption, pursuit, resistance, and cost.
2. State the dramatic question as an uncertain outcome that the ending must answer.
3. Build causality with “therefore” or “but” between major events; replace merely sequential beats.
4. Escalate by narrowing options, increasing cost, exposing vulnerability, or accelerating time pressure.
5. Place reversals that change the meaning of prior action, not only the tactical situation.

## Shape nested arcs

- Give each volume an initiating change, irreversible midpoint, crisis, climax, and transformed equilibrium.
- Make each volume resolve a major dramatic question while opening or intensifying the series question.
- Assign every chapter a local change that advances, complicates, or deliberately pauses a larger arc.
- Vary pressure across chapters; follow peaks with consequential aftermath rather than immediate repetition.

## Build chapter beats

For each chapter, define:

1. Entry condition: what has just changed and what remains unresolved.
2. Viewpoint goal: what the focal character tries to achieve now.
3. Complications: opposition that forces adaptation rather than delay alone.
4. Turn or reversal: new information, action, or cost that changes the plan.
5. Outcome: success, failure, or mixed result with a concrete consequence.
6. Exit pressure: the decision, danger, question, revelation, or emotional shift that pulls forward.

## Test each scene

- Name the scene goal, conflict, and outcome in one line each.
- Remove or combine a scene when its outcome changes nothing.
- Prefer character-caused complications over coincidence.
- Make setup earn a later payoff; make payoff alter present choices.
- Use ending hooks that arise from consequence, not withheld basic orientation.

## Deliver plotting work

Present the dramatic question, causal chain, escalation pattern, major reversals, volume arc, and chapter beats at the requested scale. Label alternatives and assumptions. Preserve author-confirmed events and identify any proposed deviation before rewriting the outline.
