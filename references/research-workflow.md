# Research Workflow

Use this reference only when research can materially improve a story decision. When research is triggered, copy `assets/research-card.md` into the author's project and complete it there; do not store project research inside the skill package.

## Research decision

Research proactively when any of the following materially affects the story:

- A real historical period, event, person, place, culture, institution, profession, technology, medical or legal practice, military practice, crime methods, folklore, or news event.
- The author asks for realism, comparable works, factual grounding, or inspiration.
- A factual answer could change plausibility or plot branches.

Skip research when the story element is fully fictional and external facts would not improve the task.

If research tools are unavailable, state that limitation, separate existing knowledge from unchecked assumptions, and do not invent sources or links.

## Queries and sources

1. Frame each query around a specific story decision rather than collecting broad background material.
2. Prefer a primary or authoritative source when one is available.
3. Support each consequential or disputed claim with at least two independent sources.
   Independence means the sources do not merely repeat the same underlying report.
4. For news, record both the publication date and the event date.
5. Put direct source links in the research card. Record enough source context to identify what each source supports.
6. Label notes as `verified facts`, `disputed claims`, or `creative extrapolation`. Do not present a disputed position as consensus.
7. State the applicable time, place, jurisdiction, and culture when those boundaries affect whether a fact applies.

## Research card procedure

1. State the story decision under `Relevance to the Story`.
2. Record only supported claims under `Verified Facts`; move uncertainty or source disagreement to `Disputed or Uncertain Claims`.
3. Keep invention under `Creative Extrapolation`, visibly separate from facts.
4. Convert findings into plot possibilities and the three adaptation directions below.
5. Review risks before drafting, then retain the direct source links used for the card.

## Creative adaptation

Produce all three directions without treating any one as the default:

### Realistic

Keep events within the strongest supported constraints and make the resulting limitations part of the drama.

### Heightened Dramatic

Intensify pressure, timing, or coincidence while naming where the version departs from typical reality.

### Transformed or Alternate

Change the setting, system, or causal premise openly so invention is not mistaken for a factual claim.

For every direction, assess anachronism, stereotype, ethics, cliché, and factual uncertainty. Flag risks that require author choice or additional research.

## Copyright boundaries

For comparable works, analyze only premise conventions, narrative structure, audience expectations, and opportunities for differentiation; do not copy prose, distinctive scenes, character designs, or living-author style. Never provide instructions intended to bypass copyright; offer high-level craft traits and original alternatives instead.
