# Character Context Layers

## Use three layers

Organize character knowledge by lifetime and authority:

1. **Canonical character dossier:** the cross-chapter authority for stable identity, formative background, behavioral causes, relationships, voice, embodiment, and arc boundaries. Main characters receive fuller dossiers; recurring supporting characters receive only durable material that will affect later scenes. One-scene roles do not require permanent dossiers.
2. **Chapter character card:** a non-canonical projection embedded in the selected chapter outline. It records how an active character enters this chapter, what they pursue, what pressure activates, what they know or misunderstand, which dossier traits should become visible, and what changes by the exit. It may cite dossier headings but must not silently redefine permanent canon.
3. **Ephemeral scene profile:** a temporary working note derived from the dossier, chapter card, present relationships, and scene pressure. It guides speech, attention, body behavior, tactics, and knowledge for the current scene. Do not promote it to canon unless the author confirms a resulting story-state update.

## Write causal dossiers

A dossier is not a biography or résumé. Record material that explains what the person notices, says, avoids, chooses, and does under pressure.

Use these stable headings:

- `Stable Core`: identity, social position, worldview, desire, need, contradiction, wound, secret, boundary, default strategy, and non-negotiable facts.
- `Deep Background`: formative home, work, class, culture, education, losses, routines, competencies, embarrassments, pleasures, and memories that can produce concrete perception or behavior.
- `Voice and Embodiment`: cadence, vocabulary, precision, hesitation, humor, evasion, listener-dependent speech, posture, movement, sensory attention, stress response, and recurring behavior.
- `Relationships`: asymmetric wants, trust, leverage, dependence, intimacy, resentment, mistaken beliefs, and latest confirmed changes.
- `Arc and Boundaries`: current arc stage, tested belief, costly choices, regression conditions, transformation evidence, and actions that require a shown cause.

Separate `confirmed`, `inferred`, and `author-planned` claims. Main characters can be detailed; recurring supporting characters should include only durable, reusable material.

## Build chapter character cards during outlining

Create one subsection per active character inside the selected chapter plan. Include `Character ID`, `Role in Chapter`, `Entry State`, `Chapter Goal`, `Pressure and Tactic`, `Knowledge Boundary`, `Activated Traits`, `Relationship Posture`, `Voice Under Pressure`, `Exit Change`, and exact `Sources`.

The card is a chapter projection, not a second source of permanent truth. Durable changes become proposed dossier or continuity updates only after chapter review and explicit author confirmation.

## Load by phase

- **Outline:** read `Stable Core`, `Relationships`, and `Arc and Boundaries` for active recurring characters. Read deep background only when the planned conflict depends on it.
- **Draft:** read the chapter card, `Stable Core`, `Voice and Embodiment`, `Relationships`, immediately previous prose, and pre-draft hard constraints. Add an exact deep-background section only when it materially shapes the chapter.
- **Revision:** reread the completed chapter and load the dossier sections and continuity evidence required for detail consistency, individuality, embodiment, language, and earned behavior.

Never read every dossier or chapter card by default. Use exact Markdown sections whenever a full file is unnecessary.

## Split continuity checks by cost

Pre-draft hard constraints are chronology, location, physical state, consequential knowledge, current relationships, capability limits, and active promises or clues directly touched by the chapter. They must be available before prose is written.

Detail consistency belongs primarily in the first revision pass: speech habits, recurring gestures, minor descriptions, background echoes, local wording, motifs, and small callbacks. Do not postpone foundational constraints, and do not load broad history for a local polish decision.
